Symbols done right

152 parts in 12 families — MOSFETs with body diode, IGBTs, GaN, thyristors, bridges, transformers with any number of secondaries, machines, sensors, control blocks — in IEC 60617 or IEEE 315 style.

Sheets inside blocks

A block is a whole sheet: open it, draw the controller, add ports — they become the block's pins. Nest as deep as your design goes.

Made for your report

LaTeX in every label, a clickable PDF with one page per sheet, SVG and PNG, and CircuiTikZ code you paste straight into your thesis.

Hierarchy

From the power stage to the PI controller

Double-click a block to go inside. The Sheets tab shows the whole tree, breadcrumbs bring you back up, and sheet ports stay connected to the pins of their block — even in the exported PDF, where every block is a link to its page.

  • Wires snap to the grid and follow parts when you move or rotate them
  • Labels turn with the part, like in LTspice
  • Your own symbols and templates, in categories you choose

Whiteboard

A whiteboard that knows electronics

Pencil with pen pressure, shapes, arrows, sticky notes, images and waveforms (PWM, ripple, step responses, three-phase…) next to your schematic. Flowcharts are built in: ISO 5807 shapes and connectors that stay attached when you move things.

  • Right-angle connectors with Yes / No labels
  • Hand-drawn look for sketches, crisp lines for reports
  • Cream notebook, white whiteboard or blackboard theme

Collaboration

Work on it together, live

Share a project with a friend, a whole team or a link: see each other's cursors and selections, comment right on the drawing, follow what someone is looking at, and go back to any earlier version.

  • Keeps working offline, syncs when you are back
  • Lock a sheet so only you can change it
  • Host it yourself for your lab or your team

Rights per sheet

Decide who sees what, sheet by sheet

Add friends by their @username, gather them in teams, then give each person or team Can edit, Can comment or Can view — on the whole project or on a single sheet. A sheet can even be hidden: it never leaves the server for that person, who only sees its block, with a padlock.

  • A student edits the controller, the power stage stays read-only
  • Keep the solution sheet hidden until the lab is over
  • Share with a team: people who join it later get access too

Presentation

Present straight from your drawing

Frames become slides. Click a block to dive into its sheet, point with the laser, write with the pen, and let your classmates follow along on their own screens.

LaTeX

Straight into your thesis

Export a sheet as CircuiTikZ code: resistors, capacitors, inductors and diodes use native CircuiTikZ symbols, every other part is drawn exactly as on screen, and your $...$ formulas stay formulas. 

\begin{circuitikz}[european]
  \draw (0,0) to[R] (1,0);
  \draw (1,0) to[cute inductor] (2.5,0);
  \node at (3,-1) {$K_p + \frac{K_i}{s}$};
\end{circuitikz}

Questions

The short answers. The long one: open the app and try — no account, nothing to install.

Is it free?

Yes. Drawing, exporting and presenting are free and need no account.

Can I work on it with my team?

Yes: add friends by @username or e-mail, make teams, and share a project with a person, a team or a link. Each one can edit, comment or view — the whole project or sheet by sheet, and a sheet can be hidden from someone. You see each other's cursors, comment on the drawing and keep a history of versions. Sharing goes through a Circuit Notebook server, which you can also run yourself.

Where are my projects saved?

In your browser, on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you share a project with a collaboration server. You can also download a project file (.olg) to back it up or move it.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once opened, the editor works without a connection; shared projects sync again when you are back online.

EU or US symbols?

Both: IEC 60617 (Europe) and IEEE 315 / ANSI (US). Switch the whole project in one click, or override a single part.

Can I put the drawings in my LaTeX report?

Yes: export a vector PDF or SVG, or CircuiTikZ code that compiles with pdflatex. All the labels can be LaTeX.

Does it simulate circuits?

No, and that's on purpose. Circuit Notebook is for drawing, explaining, presenting and working together. Keep your simulator for the numbers, and bring the waveforms back here to tell the story.
