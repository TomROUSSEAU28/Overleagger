    User manual · v0.1 beta

    How to use Circuit Notebook

    In one minute

    • Pick a part in the library (left), click on the page to place it. Press R to rotate, X to mirror.
    • Drag from a pin to draw a wire. Dots appear by themselves where three connections meet.
    • Write $V_{out}$ anywhere: it becomes LaTeX. The math bar above the text helps.
    • Draw frames around what you want to show, then Present: each frame is a slide, and things can appear click by click.
    • Your work is saved as you go. Press ? in the app for every shortcut.

    1. Getting started

    Open the app: the projects page lists your projects. Create one (New project: blank, or the example), import a .olg file, or open the buck converter example to look around: it is a small presentation that shows most of what the app can do.

    You do not need an account. Without one, projects are saved in your browser, as you go; keep a copy of important ones (Export → Project file). With an account, projects can also live on the server, so you find them on any computer and can share them (see Your account).

    • Top bar: back to the projects, the project name and where you are in its sheets, EU / US symbols, undo / redo, the grid, the three looks (cream paper, whiteboard, blackboard), the panels, Present and Export.
    • Tool rail (far left): select, pan, comments, wire, arrow, block, port, label, text, pencil, eraser, shapes, line, note, image, link button, waveform, frame, current.
    • Left panel: the library of parts, your templates, the comments and the sheets.
    • Right panel: the properties of what is selected, or of the sheet.

    Moving around: the mouse wheel moves the view (Shift + wheel sideways), Ctrl + wheel or a pinch zooms, and you can also drag with the middle button or with Space held down. F fits the drawing to the screen, 0 goes back to 100 %. Both side panels can be resized by their edge, or hidden (Ctrl \, Ctrl Shift \).

    2. Drawing a schematic

    Search the library (mosfet, transformer, pi…) or press A for the quick add box. Click a part, then click on the page to place it; it stays in your hand to place more, Esc stops. The symbol gallery shows every part, in IEC and US drawings.

    • R rotates, Shift R the other way, X / Y mirror.
    • The right panel sets the reference (R1…), the value (LaTeX works: $R_{load}$), and the options of the part: channel and body diode of a MOSFET, polarity dots and secondaries of a transformer, position of a switch, size…
    • EU / US in the top bar switches every symbol between IEC and US drawings; one part can also keep its own standard.
    • A transformer can also be drawn winding by winding (Transformer winding): put the primary and the secondaries where they belong, and give them the same reference.

    3. Wires and connections

    • Drag from a pin, or press W: click to add corners, click a pin or a wire to finish (or double-click). / flips the bend while drawing. Shift W draws a signal line with an arrow, for block diagrams.
    • A junction dot appears by itself where three or more connections meet. Wires that simply cross are not connected.
    • Moving: drag a part or a wire, and what is attached follows. A wire end on a part that stays keeps hold of its pin (the wire stretches, with square corners); a wire end teeing into another wire slides along it, with its junction dot; net labels stay on their wire.
    • One piece: click a selected wire again to pick the piece under the pointer (cut at the junctions on it). Drag it, or press Delete to remove only that piece.
    • Net labels (L): two labels with the same name are connected, even without a wire between them — also across sheets, through the block's ports.

    4. Sheets and blocks

    A block (B) is a whole sheet shown as a box: the controller of a converter, a gate driver… Double-click it (or Enter) to open its sheet; U or Esc goes back up. Inside, sheet ports (P) become the pins of the block; choose on which side of the block each one appears. Ctrl Shift B moves a selection into a new block. The breadcrumbs at the top show where you are, and the Sheets tab lists them all. A sheet can be left out of the presentation or of the PDF (its properties, with nothing selected).

    5. Text and LaTeX

    Anything between dollars is LaTeX: $\Delta i_L = \frac{V}{L}\,t$. It works in texts (T), labels, notes, shapes, arrows, values, port names and waveform labels. Double-click something (or F2) to edit its text in place.

    The math bar helps: $…$ (or Ctrl M) puts dollars around the selection or the line, and the buttons insert a fraction, an exponent, an index, a root, a bar, Greek letters and symbols (⋯ for more). Pressing the index button right after V gives $V_{n}$, ready to type over. A text can have a frame around it (box, rounded, double, underline).

    6. Whiteboard and flowcharts

    • Pencil (D) and eraser (E): freehand notes, with pen pressure; the highlighter is an option of the pencil.
    • Shapes (S): rectangle, ellipse, diamond, triangle and the flowchart shapes, with a text inside; hand-drawn look as an option.
    • Lines and arrows (Shift L): drag their middle to bend them, or give them right angles.
    • Sticky notes (N), images (I, or paste / drop a file).
    • Frames (Shift F): a named region; they are the slides of the presentation.
    • Comments (C): pin a discussion anywhere on the page; drag it to move it.

    Flowcharts

    Start or end a line on a shape: it snaps to the middle of one of its sides and stays attached, even when the shape moves. Choose Right angles in its properties for square corners, and give it a label (yes, no…).

    Links

    Anything can carry a link (right panel → Link): a web page, an online PDF (a datasheet…), or another sheet of the project. Ctrl + click follows it in the editor; a click follows it in the presentation and in the PDF. A link button (K) is a button made for that.

    7. Waveforms

    The waveform tool (O) draws oscillograms and chronograms: sine, square, PWM, triangle, sawtooth, rectified, ripple, first and second order steps, exponential, DC, or your own points. Start from a preset (buck converter, three-phase, PWM carrier, step responses…), then set each trace: amplitude, offset, periods, phase, duty cycle, colour, dashes and label (LaTeX). Stacked puts one trace per band, like a chronogram; overlay puts them on one axis. In the presentation, a trace parameter can be animated (see Animations).

    8. Arrange and style

    • Group (Ctrl G, Alt + click selects inside a group), align and distribute several things from the right panel.
    • Order: to the front, one step forward (Ctrl ]), one step backward (Ctrl [), to the back (a background).
    • Style: colour, stroke, dash and fill; Lock position so it does not move.
    • Show in: untick Presentation or Export to leave something out (draft notes, hints for you). The eye icon on the page reminds you of it.

    9. Your own parts and templates

    • New symbol (top of the library) opens the symbol editor: draw it with lines, arcs and texts, place its pins, and it joins your library. Customize symbol… in the properties of a part starts from that one.
    • Templates: select a piece of circuit (a half-bridge with its driver, a filter…) and save it as a template, in a category of your choice. Drop it in any project from the Templates tab: it stays connected.
    • Your symbols and templates are kept in your browser; with an account they follow you to every computer.

    10. Presenting

    Present (F5) shows your frames as slides, sheet by sheet; a sheet without frames is one slide. Click, → or Space goes on, ← goes back, Esc returns to the editor.

    Slides and transitions

    Slides follow the reading order of the frames. Select a frame to set its place: Slide 2 of 5 with ‹ › to show it earlier or later (the numbers appear next to the frames while one is selected; ↺ goes back to reading order). Choose its transition there too: the camera glides (default), fade, slide in, slide up, zoom through, blur or cut; fast, normal or slow.

    Overview, then zooms

    Draw a frame around the whole drawing, then smaller frames inside it: the camera flies from the overview into each part, and back. Each element plays its animations on the smallest frame around it: the overview shows the drawing, each zoom builds up its own part. Animated currents keep flowing while the camera moves.

    During the talk

    • Click a block (or a button linking to a sheet) to dive into its sheet; Backspace comes back where you were, at the same click.
    • L laser pointer, P pen (nothing is saved), C clears the pen, B blanks the screen (the button turns red while it is blank).
    • Links open in a new tab, and the presentation goes on.
    • In a shared project, the others see a Join button while you present: they follow your slides on their own screen.

    11. Animations

    Select something inside a frame and open Animation at the bottom of the right panel, then Add.

    Animation What it does
    Appear / Disappear Fade, lit-up fade, pop, rise, zoom, or wipe (an arrow draws itself). Parts of the circuit (components, wires, labels, blocks, currents) are never scaled or moved off their wires: they fade, light up or wipe.
    Emphasis Flash (the ink lights up in a colour, then comes back), glow, or for texts and shapes a gentle pulse or shake.
    Change colour The ink turns to another colour, smoothly.
    Move Slides by a distance (a marker that moves on…).
    Change state A part changes an option in place (a switch closes…), with a short flash.
    Animate the curve A waveform parameter goes from one value to another, or back and forth: a duty cycle that sweeps, a damping that improves…
    Text Typed letter by letter, or replaced by another text.
    Change the current On an animated current: its current (or offset, speed, period) goes to another value, for example from 1 A to −1 A to make it flow backwards.

    Each animation plays as the slide opens or on click 1, 2, 3…, with a duration and a delay (several on the same click start together; use the delay to play them one after the other). A click plays the next step before going to the next slide; going back undoes the last step. While the Animation section is open, small numbers on the page show on which click things happen, and a selected frame lists its build order. Preview presents that slide right away.

    Animated currents

    Show a current flowing through the circuit: dots, electrons, charges, arrows, dashes or comets that move along its path, at a speed that follows the current.

    • Select the wires (and the parts, like a resistor or a coil) the current goes through, then click Animate a current here in the right panel, or press Shift I. When they make a loop, the current goes round it.
    • Or, with nothing selected, press Shift I and click its path point by point, like a wire; click its first point to close a loop, double-click or press Enter to end it.
    • Current in amperes: a negative current flows the other way (or use Reverse). Signal: DC, sine, square, triangle (a ripple around an offset), sawtooth, PWM pulses, or a ramp, rise or decay that starts when the current appears.
    • What flows, its colour (Style), size, spacing and speed per ampere; Electron flow makes electrons go against the current.
    • It runs in the presentation, and in the editor while it is selected. Give it an Appear animation to start it on a click, or Change the current to reverse it. In exports it is drawn still (untick Show in → Export to leave it out).

    12. Export and import

    • Smart PDF: every sheet, one page each, with bookmarks; blocks and links are clickable.
    • SVG and PNG of the current sheet; Copy as image (or Shift C) to paste straight into a report, a slide or a chat.
    • CircuiTikZ: LaTeX code of the sheet, to paste in your report.
    • Project file (.olg): the whole project, to keep or send. Import it from the projects page.
    • Choose the colours (cream paper, white, blackboard…) and the background. Tick Only the selection to export just what is selected; what you left out of the export (Show in) is listed.

    13. Your account

    An account is free and optional. Sign in is at the top right of the projects page.

    • Create an account with your name, e-mail and a password: a 6-digit code is sent to your e-mail to check it (have a look in your spam folder). Or continue with GitHub, when the server offers it.
    • Forgot your password? on the sign-in window sends a code to choose a new one; it signs you out everywhere else.
    • The menu of your name: your @username (how friends find you) and your name, Friends & teams, Sign out, and Delete my account (it tells you what goes away first).
    • During the beta each account keeps a few projects on the server (shown on the projects page); projects in your browser are unlimited. Move to this computer (menu ⋯ of a project) brings one back into your browser.

    14. Working together

    • Share (top bar of a project on the server): add a friend or a whole team, as Can edit, Can comment or Can view; or send an invite link to anyone.
    • Rights per sheet: give someone more or fewer rights on one sheet, or hide a sheet from them (a lab report where each group sees its own part…).
    • Everyone's cursor and selection show live, in their colour. Comments can be answered, resolved and reopened.
    • History keeps versions of the project (automatic ones, and the ones you name): look at one, or bring it back.
    • Friends & teams (menu of your name): add friends by @username, gather them in teams, and share with a team in one click.

    15. On a phone

    On a phone the drawing takes the whole screen. The bar at the bottom opens the tools, the parts, the sheets, the comments and the settings. One finger moves the view (or a part), two fingers zoom. Tap a part to get its actions: rotate, mirror, copy, edit, delete. Presenting works too.

    16. Keyboard shortcuts

    Press ? in the app for the full list; you can change any of them there (Ctrl is ⌘ on a Mac). The most useful:
